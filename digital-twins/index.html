Series · 7 parts · August – October 2026

Building a City Digital Twin

I set out to model a city, and started where most people start - with the whole thing. This series is what the literature, the real data, and my own experiments did to that ambition, and what is left standing that is actually worth building.

The arc, in one breath

Part 1 writes down the mental model I began with - a city as entities on geography, flows between them, and senses that watch both - then records the six corrections the literature and the data forced on it: senses that report at speeds seven orders of magnitude apart, sensors that are a shadow of the state rather than a mirror of it, people who re-optimise rather than drift like particles, single runs that hide their own uncertainty, governance that is the controller of the loop rather than another entity inside it, and an output that should compare futures instead of forecasting one. Every correction pushes the same direction - toward a smaller, scoped twin. Part 2 puts that argument on trial against the record: a decade of government everything-twins that each converged to a scoped survivable core - a geometry platform, a flood demonstrator, a framework document - the scoped products that shipped and stayed shipped, and a US$2 billion foundation-model company running the same experiment today, whose independent validators have already drawn the envelope around its scoped part. It closes on the capability nobody has built: a spatial twin coupled to a council’s money. Part 3 stops arguing and goes to stand inside one of the twins the record vindicated: the NSW Spatial Digital Twin. On census night it takes the portal up on its “add your own data” button and pushes a census-night headcount down onto individual land parcels, a grain the Bureau of Statistics never publishes at, which turns out to be a lesson in what a scoped twin will and will not tell you. Part 4 stops reading and starts building: a scoped twin made by hand, with OpenStreetMap roads cropped by osmium and run through SUMO, put to one concrete question - how long the Victoria Park precinct takes to clear when 63,000 people leave the Brisbane 2032 stadium at once, and where it chokes. Thirteen real runs are baked into an interactive map, with the assumptions and the sanity checks set out beside them. Part 5 follows the upgrade path Part 4 named, on the build that had been running alongside it: a population drawn from the census that chooses its own trips on one MATSim engine, every input on a ledger, held to twelve real ridership figures and reported honestly at two of twelve. Part 6 is how that build was made: 64 sessions with AI coding agents that remembered nothing between sessions, a repository kept as their table of contents, and mistakes promoted into checks - and the proof it transfers, Mumbai on the same framework in days. Part 7 is what Mumbai led to: the proposal we prepared for a state government in India, published in full, and an honest map of which parts are the running twin, which are real ground under illustrative layers, and which are a direction of travel.
